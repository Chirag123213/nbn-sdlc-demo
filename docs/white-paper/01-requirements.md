## 1. Overview

The project requirements define how a developer prepares a task, uses AI to build a feature, checks the result and obtains human approval. They also cover shared prompts, security safeguards, usage tracking and responsibility for AI-generated work. We developed these requirements from Alessio's brief, the research and two developer interviews, then used acceptance criteria to assess the fault-reporting and technician-dispatch features. The following sections link each recorded brief requirement to its answering chapter and distinguish demonstrated results from incomplete checks and unresolved decisions.

#### Scope and requirement changes

The [requirements file][requirements] develops in three passes. Pass 1 establishes lifecycle responsibilities, verification and safeguards. Pass 2 adds commit, review, secret-scanning, test-strength and deployment requirements. The Sprint 2 backlog adds ready issues, effort/usage recording and shared prompts. Pass 3 incorporates governance and interview findings, then expresses the fault-reporting criteria in Given/When/Then form. [PR #56][requirements-pr] merged that third pass on 28 Sep 2026.

The [interview findings][interviews] support clear business context, scoped AI work, human validation and human responsibility. The organisations described different levels of autonomy and different ways of recording AI involvement. The team's provenance rules are therefore a governance choice, not a description of one universal practice. The absence of junior interviewees limits conclusions about the intended reader's needs.

[ADR-002][scope] records the decision to cover development and testing in depth, planning and deployment at their handoffs, and assume stages 1 to 4 have supplied suitable inputs. It remains labelled Proposed, despite being merged. Its account of the client hearing the focus without objection does not establish formal approval of every detail. The wider brief remains visible in the traceability table rather than being silently reduced to the two feature builds.

The [review index][review] reports **12/14 feature criteria for A, 12/14 for B and 14/14 for C**, including the qualified keyboard pass. Evidence criteria AC15 to AC17 are outside that denominator. The feature totals therefore do not mean all seventeen criteria are unconditionally met.

The frozen [AC01][criteria] asks a requester to create a dispatch request; AC11 asks a scheduled dispatch to record the selected technician, visit details and matched requirement. The brief does not explicitly state that records must survive a refresh, name a database or require a history screen. A/B's builder records interpret the scope as permitting an in-memory flow. The [test protocol][protocol] uses a stronger operational reading: TJ01 checks visibility afterwards and TJ11 checks the stored record. The reviewer therefore failed A/B on AC01 and AC11 while the builders had marked them passed.

This chapter preserves both the frozen wording and the reviewer's result. Sidney Zeng is the decision owner named in the lane records; Ahmed supplies the requirements wording and Zac the test interpretation. The acceptance decision needs to state whether durable storage was a clarification or an added expectation and apply the same reasoning to all builds. A later decision or correction must not replace the original frozen record.

### 3.4 What this does not show

The trials contain one build per method and one builder per method. They cannot separate a builder's choices from the method or establish a general ranking. Repository setup also differed: moves/forks did not retain every ruleset, as recorded in the [protocol amendments][protocol].

The review's feature passes do not demonstrate every original SDLC requirement. For example, UI accessibility checks do not establish the full visual-baseline requirement in US-15; a mutation score does not establish an adopted score threshold under US-14; and a preview does not establish production release or rollback. Missing measurements remain unavailable.

The brief table uses the team's proposal and recorded brief account. The original brief and original showcase spreadsheet were not independently inspected. Proposed chapter destinations describe where material belongs; they are not a claim that the assembled white paper, dual-audience learning path or final handover is complete.

### 3.5 What the manual takes from it

For **task 5.3**, write down what creating and recording data means before implementation: whether it survives refresh, who can retrieve it and the example that proves this. Agree the acceptance check with the requirement, keeping additional attack tests and evidence-format rules separate.

For **task 6.1**, give the developer a ready issue with business context, criteria, exclusions and the agreed starting revision. Record clarifications before a comparison starts rather than supplying different interpretations afterwards.

For **tasks 7.1 and 7.4**, link each criterion to an actual check and reachable evidence. Distinguish live, mocked and incomplete checks. Passing generated tests or a builder's screenshot is supporting evidence rather than final acceptance.

For **task 6.4**, record the reviewer's decision, disagreements and the person responsible for resolving them. Keep original results and later corrections traceable so the next reader can understand why the outcome changed.

## 6. New terms

| Term            | Meaning                                                                                                                                         |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Traceability    | The link between a requested outcome, its criterion, the check, the evidence and the acceptance decision.                                       |
| Durable storage | A record retained beyond the current browser page state. The required retention and retrieval behaviour should be agreed before implementation. |
